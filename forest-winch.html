EPFL course project

Forest winch

Context
Mechanical Construction II, EPFL (bachelor in microengineering)
Team
4 students, with Yann Royer
Max pull
Up to 3.5 kN
Gearing
15:1 (1:3, then 1:5)
Cable
6 mm, safety factor 5
Grade
6/6

Render

A hand-cranked winch for pulling logs out of the forest. One person turns the crank and the cable pulls up to 3.5 kN. We were four students, and we built it for the Mechanical Construction II course at EPFL.

The gearing has two stages, 1:3 and then 1:5, so 15:1 from the 250 mm crank to the drum. A ratchet on the drum holds the load when you let go of the handle, and a small lever lifts the pawl to release it. For lighter logs the crank can move to the intermediate shaft, which gives 5:1 and winds three times faster.

To size everything we measured instead of guessing. We turned a bike with a power meter by hand to see what a person can put out, about 55 W, which is around 44 N at the handle, and we dragged a log along the ground to get its friction (1.19 static, 0.98 moving). The worst slope needs 3537 N on the cable, and we checked every part against that: shafts, keys, gear teeth, bearings, drum, cable screws, ratchet, hooks and straps. Overall efficiency comes out around 90%. Winding 20 m of cable takes about 16 minutes.

Made with Yann Royer
