EPFL course project

FlyForce

Team
5 students, with Yann Royer
Grade
5.25/6
Status
Designed and sized on paper, not built
Size
179.5 mm × Ø84 mm, 810 g
Resolution
0.3 mN

Images

FlyForce is a 3-axis force sensor for a measuring probe. It is made only of flexure hinges, and it is balanced so the robot's own accelerations don't show up in the measurement. We were five students on it, for the Mechanism Design II course at EPFL.

A 200 mm stylus with a sapphire ball touches the part. The sensor has to read the contact force along x, y and z (±5 N, resolution under 1.5 mN, stiffness 5 N/mm) while the robot carrying it accelerates at up to 6 m/s² and turns at up to 5 rad/s. The guiding had to be flexures only, so no friction and no play.

The stylus sits on a vertical bar that tilts around a point P for x and y, and moves along z on three arms set 120° apart. Four counterweights keep the centre of mass on P, so gravity and linear accelerations don't move the stylus, and the symmetry of the arms cancels the moments. Three Micro-Epsilon capacitive sensors read the displacement.

We wrote the stiffness of the whole mechanism from the strain energy of its 21 hinges, then tuned each one until the stiffness stayed at 5 N/mm in every direction. They are all steel notch hinges, between 0.08 and 0.63 mm thick, and each one stays under its fatigue limit for 10 years of use.

The sensor fits in a 179.5 mm × Ø84 mm cylinder and weighs 810 g, against a limit of 1 kg. Force resolution is 0.3 mN, and the natural frequency is 94 Hz in the xy plane and 14 Hz along z. One requirement is missed: the counterweights are heavy, so spinning the sensor around z at 5 rad/s causes an error of up to 0.15 N. To stay under 15 mN, that rotation has to be limited to 1.6 rad/s.

With only notch hinges, each arm is a flat part cut in one pass by wire EDM. The central shaft is turned, then milled. The flexures are Böhler K190 steel, the counterweights 42CrMo4 and the rest 7075 aluminium.

Made with Yann Royer
