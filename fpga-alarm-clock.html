EPFL course project

FPGA alarm clock

Context
Logic Systems, EPFL (bachelor in microengineering)
Team
2 students, with Yann Royer
Grade
6/6
Board
DE10-Lite
Tools
Logisim

Images

An alarm clock built from logic gates on a DE10-Lite FPGA board, with two alarms, a stopwatch, a timer, a metronome and a music player. We were two students, and we made it for the Logic Systems course at EPFL.

A menu button cycles through eight functions, and a rotary encoder with a built-in button sets the values, with the digit being set blinking. Each of the two alarms rings with a buzzer or one of three tunes. The metronome blinks an LED and beeps, and its tempo can be set with the encoder or by tapping it in.

Everything is drawn in Logisim from gates, flip-flops, counters and ROMs, then loaded onto the FPGA. A 16-state machine holds the eight functions and their menu entries. Each button goes through two D flip-flops in a row, which turns a press of any length into a pulse of exactly one clock tick. Hours, minutes and seconds are binary numbers, converted to decimal digits for the seven-segment displays with the Double Dabble algorithm.

The 24 notes over two octaves come from 24 clocks at different frequencies. Each tune is a ROM with one note per word, read by a counter running at the tempo of the tune. Converting between BPM and period needs a division, but Logisim's divider doesn't run on the FPGA and our own 16-bit divider was too large. Since the dividend is constant, we replaced it with a 256-entry lookup table.

Made with Yann Royer
